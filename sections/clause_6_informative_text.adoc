== Introduction

The STAplus 1.1 Standard extends <<OGC22-022r2>> with two entity types that capture observer context.

STAplus 1.0.1 associates observations with a `Party` through a `Datastream` or `MultiDatastream`. That association expresses ownership. It does not record where the party was, or what the party was doing, when the observation was made. SensorThings already has `Location` (the location of a `Thing`) and `FeatureOfInterest` (the real-world object or area that was observed). Neither of those entity types is the observer's position.

Citizen Science and similar multi-user deployments often need that observer context. A mobile contributor may observe a feature at a distance, so the party's position is not the `FeatureOfInterest`. The same contributor may record different activities (for example walking, cycling, or stationary sampling) that help interpret the observation.

STAplus 1.1 therefore adds:

- **PartyLocation**: the position of the party, with time, optional elevation and uncertainty, an environment code, optional `retentionTime`, and optional `restricted`. An `Observation` has zero-or-one `PartyLocation`. A `Party` has zero-to-many `PartyLocations`. The implementation advertises the accepted `environment` EnvCodes on the landing page.
- **PartyActivity**: the activity the party was performing, identified by a URI `definition` and a time instant or period. An `Observation` has zero-or-one `PartyActivity`. A `Party` has zero-to-many `PartyActivities`.
- A `PartyLocation` and a `PartyActivity` can each be reused among many observations. That reuse requires the association with `Party`.
- A `PartyLocation` and a `PartyActivity` may be linked to each other. That association allows user context (where the party was and what the party was doing) to be determined without involving observations. This association is beyond the scope of <<ISO19156>>, whose data model is observation-centric.

`FeatureOfInterest` has no `Party` association. It is therefore a public resource. A client that creates a `FeatureOfInterest` asserts that the feature is a publicly observable object or area (for example a park or a lake), not a personal location, and that `feature` geometry is not the observing user's position. If that geometry would be the observing user's position, it is null and the position is stored in `PartyLocation`.

=== Sharing personal context

`PartyLocation` and `PartyActivity` can be personal data. The STAplus 1.0.1 Business Logic advertises a GDPR compliance note for `Thing` location of an individual party. The <<conformance_class_gdpr>> Conformance Class of this Standard is the full GDPR class: it restricts those `Thing` locations and additionally restricts Read, Create, Update, and Delete of `PartyLocation` and `PartyActivity` to the authenticated owner, blocks leak through `$expand`, navigation, deep insert, and MQTT, and specifies retention, bulk erasure, portability of the party graph, and restriction. An implementation that accepts a `Party` whose `role` is equal to `individual` also claims that Conformance Class. That default is not sufficient when a contributor wants named other parties to see, or in some cases to update, that context. Examples include a campaign coordinator who reviews observer tracks, or a research group that interprets observations together with the activity the observer was performing.

STAplus already has concepts that look similar and are not this kind of grant:

- **Ownership** (`Party`) identifies who owns an entity. It is zero-or-one. It is not a list of readers.
- **License** expresses reuse of data that is already readable. It does not unlock a GDPR-locked `PartyLocation` or `PartyActivity`.
- **ObservationGroup** packages observations for reuse. It has no recipients.
- **Business Logic** can narrow access further. It cannot widen access. Named grants are a `Share`, not Business Logic.

STAplus 1.1 therefore adds the optional <<conformance_class_sharing_party_context>> Conformance Class and the `Share` entity. A `Share` is a grant from a party (the grantor) to one or more other parties (the recipients) of a permission on exactly one target. The grantor is the `Party` of the target. The grant records `permission` (`read` or `update`; `update` includes `read`), a mandatory `purpose`, a `creationTime`, an optional `endTime`, and an optional `description`. Deleting the `Share` or reaching `endTime` revokes the grant.

A `Share` targets exactly one of `PartyLocation` or `PartyActivity` (`count(partyLocation)+count(partyActivity)==1`). That keeps the grant selective. Access to a location does not imply access to an activity, and the reverse is also true. A grant can apply to one activity at one location, or to one location for an activity. To share both, the grantor creates two `Share` entities. To share several location-activity pairs, the grantor creates one `Share` per entity of the pair. One `Share` can name many recipients, so the same grant need not be repeated for each recipient.

Recipients do not inherit access through `$expand` or a navigationLink. Sharing an activity does not expose the associated locations unless those locations are themselves the target of a valid `Share`. Recipients never create, re-share, or delete another party's `PartyLocation` or `PartyActivity`. Delete of the target remains with the grantor. Update of the target is allowed only when `permission` is `update`, and a recipient cannot change the owning `Party`.

Implementations that want open `PartyLocation` and `PartyActivity` keep Party Context and omit GDPR only when no `Party` has `role` equal to `individual` (for example autonomously acting systems). Implementations that accept individual parties implement GDPR. Implementations that want private entities plus named grants implement GDPR and Sharing Party Context together. Conformance to GDPR is not a certification of compliance with <<GDPR>>. The remaining controller obligations are listed in <<annex-gdpr>>.
