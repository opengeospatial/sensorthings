[[clause-gdpr]]
== Requirements Class GDPR

=== Overview

The GDPR requirements class applies to a STAplus implementation that processes entities attributable to Parties whose `role` is `individual`. It adds the entity type `Agreement` and constrains how the implementation stores and releases the personal data identified in <<clause-analysis>>.

=== Party roles

The requirements of this Requirements Class apply to entities attributable to a Party whose `role` is `individual`. A Party with the `role` `institutional` or `system` takes its entities out of that scope. The role therefore must not be chosen by the principal itself: only identities managed by the operator can be institutions or systems, and every other principal is an individual. An identity managed by the operator can also be an individual, for example a citizen who has a local account.

include::../requirements/gdpr/REQ001a_party-role.adoc[]

NOTE: A typical deployment accepts two authentication sources. Citizens authenticate with an external OAuth 2.0 or OpenID Connect provider; their Parties are always `individual`. Institutions, such as a municipal environmental office, and autonomous systems, such as the station network of an operator or an aggregation service, authenticate with local accounts that the operator creates, for example with HTTP Basic authentication over TLS; their Parties are `institutional` or `system`. The operator can also create local accounts for citizens who do not use the external provider; their Parties are `individual`, like those of external identities. `Party/authId` is qualified by the source, for example `https://login.example.org#4711` for an external subject and `local:station-network` for a local account. If the implementation claims the Accepted Clients conformance class, the operator binds each local account to the Client of the software release it runs, so that the credential resolves to both the principal and the client release (<<req_accepted-clients_client-acceptance>>). A sensor that belongs to a citizen and writes autonomously is not a system in this sense: it writes as the `individual` Party of its owner, with a credential bound to the owner and to the Client of its firmware release.

=== add:[Entity type ReleaseProfile]

add:[The `ReleaseProfile` entity type records the conditions under which a data subject's personal data are released to others: to whom (`audience` and `Recipients`), through which applications (`clientIds`, Accepted Clients conformance class), at what precision (`locationResolution`, `timeResolution`, and `zoneLevel` with the DGGS conformance class), and where a minimum obfuscation applies whatever is authorized (`privacyAreas`, and `privacyZones` with the DGGS conformance class). The Agreement records the legal authorization; the ReleaseProfile records how an authorized release takes place. Together they form the record of what the data subject agreed to. Both are immutable, and both are visible only to their data subject.]

add:[A ReleaseProfile has two roles. Its `audience`, `Recipients`, `clientIds` and resolutions take effect only through an effective Agreement that is associated with it; several Agreements can share one ReleaseProfile. Its `privacyAreas` take effect as soon as it is created, whether or not an Agreement is associated with it, and restrict every release of the data subject's locations and times. A ReleaseProfile without Agreements therefore expresses a pure restriction, for example "`never release my locations near my home finer than 1 km`". A privacy area is lifted by deleting its ReleaseProfile.]

By default, and in accordance with data protection by default (GDPR Article 25(2)), nothing covered by the register is released with full precision.

add:[The precision at which locations and times are released to others is determined per property value and per requester, from three sources: the public generalization advertised by the operator, the ReleaseProfiles of the effective Agreements (grants), and the privacy areas of the data subject (floors). <<fig-release-resolution>> summarizes the determination; with the DGGS conformance class, <<fig-release-level>> applies to locations instead.]

[[fig-release-resolution]]
.add:[Determination of the release resolution for a requester that is not the data subject]
[source]
----
G  = resolution of the advertised locationGeneralization or timeGeneralization
grants = ReleaseProfiles of the Agreements effective for (property path, publish, requester)
if grants exist:
      R = min(locationResolution or timeResolution of the grants; absent counts as 0)
      R = min(R, G)                                  (if G is advertised)
else: R = G                                          (if G is absent: withheld or omitted)
for each privacy area of any ReleaseProfile of the Party that applies to the value:
      R = max(R, locationResolution or timeResolution of the privacy area)
release: stored value, if R = 0; generalized value at R, if R > 0 and G is advertised;
         otherwise withheld (mandatory property) or omitted (optional property)
----
